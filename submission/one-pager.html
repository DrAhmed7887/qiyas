Qiyas قياس

An Arabic-first child growth check for parents · the measurement, explained

UnivaBio 2026 · AI for Human Health · solo entry

WHO did the math. Parents need the meaning.

Problem

The WHO Child Growth Standards (2006) define how a child's weight and length compare with healthy children worldwide. WHO's own tools (WHO Anthro, the Anthro Survey Analyser) are built for health workers and population surveys, not for a parent with a tape measure. A common, silent error makes things worse: under age two WHO measures lying down, and a child measured standing reads about 0.7 cm short, enough to move a result across a cut-off.

What Qiyas does

  • Runs in the browser, for one visit: no install, no account, nothing stored.
  • A parent enters date of birth, sex, weight, length or height, and how the child was measured.
  • Computes weight-for-age, length/height-for-age and weight-for-length/height z-scores (0–5 years) from WHO's own tables, applies WHO's 0.7 cm lying/standing correction, and flags implausible values instead of showing them.
  • Explains the result in plain Arabic (default, right-to-left) or English, says when to see a health worker, and shows its working: entered → adjusted → WHO table row → z-score → percentile, including what the result would have read without the correction.
  • Three warning-sign questions (e.g. swelling of both feet). Any "yes" shows "see a health worker today" first, even if every number needs re-measuring. No diagnosis labels, ever.

How it works

Inputs: date of birth, sex, weight, length, lying / standing, 3 warning signs
Deterministic engine: WHO expanded LMS tables, 731-day rules, restricted z beyond ±3
WHO implausibility flags · fixed band · independent urgent-action rule
Physician-reviewed Arabic / English text
Optional AI: one fixed question, answered only from the approved text, output filtered; it cannot change the result

Evidence & safety

  • Data: WHO expanded tables, downloaded from WHO, values unmodified (sources in data/who/SOURCE.md).
  • 134 automated tests, all passing. WHO's own table rows are fed back through the engine and must return −3 … +3, plus the restricted method, the 730/731-day boundaries, every flag edge, every input error and the warning-sign override.
  • No AI in the maths. No storage, analytics or logs of inputs. Synthetic examples only.
  • Clinical wording reviewed and signed off by Dr. Ahmed Zayed (physician, 12 years' practice).
  • Related evidence: in a randomised trial, a parent-facing growth app (KhunLook, JMIR 2023) raised high health literacy from 51.6% to 59.9%. Context for a related app, not a Qiyas outcome.

Prior art, credited

WHO Anthro and the Anthro Survey Analyser (health workers, surveys). UNICEF's Bebbo offers parents WHO growth bands; its Arabic was removed in April 2025 and it needs an install and a child profile. Qiyas is not the first growth app: it is a one-visit measurement check that corrects how the child was measured and shows its working.

Finished vs planned

  • Finished: 3 WHO indicators, ages 0–5, measurement correction, implausibility flags, warning-sign override, Arabic + English, calculation view, optional bounded AI (local server).
  • Not in this build: BMI-for-age, ages 5–19, tracking over visits, native app. The hosted demo runs without the AI server and shows the approved text.
